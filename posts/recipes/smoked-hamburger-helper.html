Smoked Hamburger Helper

Cheesy, beefy shells with a hint of smoke, made on the pellet grill.

Smoked Hamburger Helper

Idea by KING-D0RK, who shared it in a forum post. I wrote out the full steps here.

🧪 Kitchen-test in progress: I haven't made this one yet, so let me know how it turns out!

Prep time15 min
Cook time2 hr 15 min
TotalAbout 2½ hr
Serves6

Ingredients

  • Beef and sauce:
  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1 (6 oz) can tomato paste
  • 1 (10.5 oz) can cream of mushroom soup
  • 1 bag (about 12 oz) frozen chopped onion
  • ¼ cup heavy cream, plus a few splashes to finish if needed
  • 1 (16 oz) block Velveeta, cubed
  • Seasoning:
  • 1 tablespoon minced garlic
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning (or more to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika (or more to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder (or more to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder (or more to taste)
  • Pasta:
  • 1 box (16 oz) medium shell pasta
  • Salt for the pasta water

Steps

  1. Heat the smoker: Preheat your smoker or pellet grill to 200°F. A mild wood like hickory, oak or a competition blend works well with beef and cheese.
  2. Brown the beef: Cook the ground beef in a large skillet over medium-high heat, breaking it up, until no pink remains, about 8 minutes. Drain off most of the fat.
  3. Build the pan: Spread the beef in a 9×13-inch disposable foil pan or a 12-inch cast iron skillet. Add the frozen onion (no need to thaw), tomato paste, cream of mushroom soup, ¼ cup heavy cream, minced garlic and all the seasonings. Stir until the tomato paste is mixed in, then scatter the Velveeta cubes over the top.
  4. Smoke: Set the pan in the smoker uncovered and smoke at 200°F for 2 hours. Stir every 30 to 45 minutes so the cheese melts evenly and the edges don't dry out.
  5. Cook the pasta: About 20 minutes before the smoke is done, boil the shells in well-salted water until just al dente, 1 to 2 minutes less than the box says. Drain, saving about 1 cup of the pasta water.
  6. Combine: Pull the pan from the smoker and stir until the sauce is smooth. Fold in the drained shells until every piece is coated.
  7. Adjust and serve: If it looks thick, stir in a few splashes of heavy cream or a little pasta water until saucy. Taste, add more seasoning if you like, and serve hot.

Pro tips

  • Smoker notes: Keep the pan uncovered so it soaks up smoke. Place it on the middle rack away from hot spots, and if your smoker runs hot, check and stir more often.
  • Pan choice: A foil pan makes cleanup easy. Cast iron holds heat well and keeps it warm at the table.
  • Tip: Cook the pasta just shy of done. It keeps soaking up sauce after you stir it in, so it won't turn mushy. Leftovers thicken in the fridge, so loosen them with a splash of cream or milk when reheating.
