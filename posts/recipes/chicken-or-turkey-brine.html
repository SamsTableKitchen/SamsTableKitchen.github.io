Chicken or Turkey Brine

My house brine mix. I keep a jar ready for smoking and roasting chicken or turkey. Use it for my Smoked Whole BBQ Chicken.

Prep time10 min
Brine timeAbout 1 hr per lb, or overnight
MakesAbout 670 g mix
This recipe makes extra seasoning. For a chicken brine, only use 1 cup of the brine mix per gallon of water. Store the rest in an airtight jar.

Ingredients

  • Brine mix (makes about 670 g):
  • 400 g kosher salt (about 1⅔ cups Morton or 3 cups Diamond Crystal)
  • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar
  • 30 g (3 tablespoons) granulated garlic
  • 15 g (2 tablespoons) coarse black pepper
  • 10 g (1½ tablespoons) onion powder
  • 6 g (1 tablespoon) paprika
  • 4 g (2 teaspoons) dried thyme
  • 3 g (1 teaspoon) rubbed sage
  • 2 g (1 teaspoon) celery seed

Steps

  1. Make the mix: Mix everything together and store it airtight. Weigh the salt if you can, since kosher salt brands differ a lot by volume.
  2. Make the brine: For chicken, stir 1 cup of the mix into 1 gallon of water or chicken broth. For a turkey, use the whole batch in 1 gallon of liquid, or enough to fully cover the bird.
  3. Dissolve and chill: If you like, warm the liquid to help dissolve the mix, then chill it completely before adding the poultry.
  4. Brine: Brine about 1 hour per pound, or overnight, in the fridge in a food-safe container or brine bag.
  5. Rinse and dry: Rinse the poultry well and pat it completely dry before smoking or roasting.

Pro tips

  • How much it makes: One batch is enough for one turkey or 2 to 3 chicken brines.
  • Swap: If I only have celery salt, I use 2 teaspoons (about 10 g) celery salt instead of the celery seed and cut the kosher salt from 400 g to 395 g.
  • Optional extras: Add whole peppercorns, citrus slices or herbs to the brine.
