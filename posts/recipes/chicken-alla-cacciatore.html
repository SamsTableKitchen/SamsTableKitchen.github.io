Chicken alla Cacciatore

Adapted from a CSN culinary class recipe.

Prep time15 min
Cook time1 hour
Total1 hour 15 min
Serves1–2

Times are estimates based on the steps.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb bone-in chicken drumsticks and thighs
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3/8 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon canola oil
  • 1/2 onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/3 green bell pepper, cut into thin strips
  • 1/3 to 1/2 celery stalk, cut into thin strips
  • 1/3 to 1/2 large carrot, finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon white wine
  • 2/3 cup crushed canned tomatoes
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 small bay leaf
  • 1/2 teaspoon fresh basil, thinly sliced
  • 1/3 cup water

Steps

  1. Mix the flour with the salt and pepper. Coat the chicken in the flour and shake off the extra.
  2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over high heat. Brown the chicken well on all sides, then move it to a plate, or into a small Dutch oven or other oven-safe pan with a lid if that's what you'll finish it in.
  3. Turn the heat down to low. Add the onion, bell pepper, celery, carrot, and garlic to the same skillet. Cook, stirring now and then, until the vegetables are almost tender.
  4. Stir in the wine, crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, bay leaf, basil, and water. Bring it to a boil, then let it simmer about 5 minutes.
  5. Pour the sauce over the chicken. Bring it back to a boil, then cover and cook in a 300°F oven, or over low heat on the stove, for 30 to 45 minutes, until the chicken is tender and reaches 165°F inside.
  6. Transfer the chicken to a platter. Skim the fat off the sauce, then boil the sauce over high heat until it thickens to how you like it. Taste and add salt and pepper if it needs it, then pour the sauce over the chicken.

Notes

For one batch I use 2 drumsticks and 1 thigh, about a pound.

I add about 1/3 cup water with the tomatoes so there's enough sauce to braise the chicken.

After the chicken is tender, I take it out, skim the fat off the sauce, and reduce it until it looks right, then pour it back over the chicken.

A small piece of a bay leaf is enough if yours are large.
